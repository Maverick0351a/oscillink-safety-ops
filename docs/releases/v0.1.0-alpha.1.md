This candidate packages the first bounded Safety Ops evidence contracts and offline evaluation paths
for private inspection. It is not a public release and does not authorize a push, tag, GitHub release,
visibility change, deployment, or announcement.

- Exact-byte physical-intelligence evidence envelopes.
- Safety Evidence Packet v1 with explicit unknowns and fixed no-authority states.
- Official-source regulatory evidence and bounded source-change lineage.
- Metadata-only licensed-standard governance.
- Read-only operational evidence normalization and external review ledgers.
- Recorded-episode evidence and offline finding generation.

- Deterministic repository visuals, synthetic demonstration, and publication gates.

## Verification required before promotion

The implementation-candidate results are recorded in the
[release-candidate verification report](../audits/release-candidate-0.1.0a1.md).

The exact candidate commit must pass:

1. Windows canonical verification and both pytest invocation forms.
2. A portable release-manifest checksum round-trip in an isolated directory.
3. Complete reachable-history and dependency scans.
4. Detached exact-SHA verification on the independent Linux Buildbox.
5. Source-distribution and wheel-payload comparison across hosts.
6. Hosted Windows and Linux CI after an explicitly authorized private push.

A passing local or Buildbox run is reproducibility evidence only. It is not practitioner validation,
legal review, regulatory approval, engineering approval, certification, safe-operation evidence, or
production-readiness evidence.

## Authority and use limitations

Safety Ops does not:

- determine legal or regulatory applicability;
- determine, guarantee, or certify compliance;
- authorize work, permits, lockout/tagout, or operation;
- resolve conflicts among regulations, standards, manuals, procedures, permits, or task plans;
- replace qualified safety, legal, engineering, operational, or regulatory reviewers; or
- command robots, machines, vehicles, PLCs, interlocks, emergency stops, or actuators.

All included examples are project-authored synthetic evidence unless a source is explicitly identified
otherwise. Licensed standards remain metadata-only until lawful storage and processing rights are
confirmed.
