# Practitioner interview protocol

## Purpose

Test whether safety-document applicability and reconciliation is a repeated, costly workflow—not
whether people like an AI safety demo.

## Participant record

Keep private:

- anonymous participant code;
- role, industry, jurisdiction, and workflow;
- date and protocol revision;
- documents/systems involved, without uploading private content;
- observed process, timing, failures, and current workaround;
- authority owner and budget owner;
- willingness to run a local read-only report; and
- consent/publication status.

## Interview prompts

1. Pick one real maintenance, inspection, integration, or robot-cell task.
2. Which regulation, standard, manual, SOP, risk assessment, work order, permit, label, and training
   records govern it?
3. How do you determine the exact asset/model/serial and current procedure revision?
4. What happens when sources disagree or a document is missing?
5. Which steps require an authorized person rather than a checklist?
6. How are procedure/manual changes propagated to task plans, training, simulation, or evaluation?
7. What remains paper, PDF, image, email, or tribal knowledge?
8. Which false positive or missed requirement would make an evidence tool unusable?
9. What evidence must stay local/private/licensed?
10. Would you run a local report comparing one asset, task, manual, and SOP this week?
11. Who owns the workflow, liability, and budget?

Do not ask participants to send proprietary documents during discovery. A later local pilot can
process private evidence without uploading it.

## Evidence threshold

Proceed to the contract fixture only if:

- five practitioners recognize the bundle;
- at least three report repeated revision/applicability/conflict work;
- at least two can provide a sanitized or local-only pilot example;
- at least one identifies a recurring workflow and budget owner; and
- the need is not adequately solved by an existing CMMS/work-instruction export plus a trivial
  structured check.

A failed gate means narrow or stop. It does not justify adding OCR or physical-control scope.
